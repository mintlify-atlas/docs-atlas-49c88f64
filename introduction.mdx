AI Smart Glasses is an open-source, Linux-based smart glasses platform designed for developers who want to build real-world AR applications on affordable, hackable hardware. The platform runs on a Rockchip RV1106B (ARM Cortex-A7) SoC and exposes camera, audio, GPIO, and display hardware through a C SDK — giving you full control from bare-metal peripherals up to application logic. Whether you're building a clinical assistant, an industrial inspection tool, or an educational AR guide, the platform provides a complete starting point under the Apache 2.0 license.

## Platform overview

The open-source scope covers the system software and application stack. You can develop on any RV1106B-based development board by adapting the peripherals to your own hardware design, or use the official AI glasses kit for a faster path with pre-wired peripherals and preloaded firmware.

## Hardware options

The project supports two development paths:

- **Your own RV1106B board** — Use any RV1106B-based development board and adapt the peripheral layer to your hardware design. This is the fully open-source path.
- **Official AI glasses kit** — The integrated kit ships with pre-wired peripherals and factory firmware. Recommended for faster bring-up when you want to focus on application development rather than hardware integration.

## The ai-core service

The firmware ships with an optional bundled binary called `ai-core`, which runs as a system service and centrally manages camera, microphone, speaker, display, and GPIO. It is **not required** for open-source development.

You can build your application entirely against the public SDK — camera, GPIO, audio, and IPC — without depending on `ai-core`. If your application's requirements match the built-in workflow, developing on top of `ai-core` is usually simpler and faster. If you prefer full control, you can disable it by removing or renaming `/etc/init.d/S53_guard` and rebooting.

## Use cases

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Medical applications" icon="stethoscope">
    **Patient information display** — Doctors and nurses see patient name, bed number, diagnosis, allergy history, and key vitals in the corner of their field of view upon entering a ward, without consulting a computer.

    **Real-time vital signs monitoring** — The glasses integrate data from bedside monitors. Abnormal heart rate, blood oxygen, or blood pressure triggers a highlighted warning in the field of view with an audio alert.

    **Operation and procedure verification** — The camera scans drug barcodes and patient wristbands during infusion or medication administration to verify dosage and patient identity, preventing medical errors before they happen.

    **Remote expert collaboration** — Junior doctors share live first-person video with remote specialists, who annotate the shared stream with AR markers and voice guidance — as if they were present on site.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Industrial applications" icon="industry">
    **Substation operations** — The glasses read paper or electronic operation tickets, recognize on-site equipment through the camera, check commands against safety rules, and issue voice warnings if an operator approaches the wrong equipment.

    **Remote maintenance guidance** — On-site technicians share first-person video with backend expert teams. Experts annotate the live stream with AR overlays — circles, arrows, and text — directly projected into the field worker's field of view.

    **Multi-party consultation** — Multiple experts join a single video session for complex fault diagnosis. Sessions are recorded and archived to build maintenance case libraries for future training.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Educational applications" icon="graduation-cap">
    **AR operation guidance** — Complex SOPs are broken into step-by-step AR instructions superimposed on real equipment. The current step is highlighted, and the system automatically advances when completion is detected visually.

    **Tool and material recognition** — The camera recognizes whether the operator has picked up the correct tool for the current step, issuing warnings before equipment damage or assembly errors can occur.

    **Voice navigation** — When both hands are occupied, operators control guidance playback with voice commands: "next," "previous," "repeat" — keeping full attention on the task.

    **New employee training** — AR guidance reduces onboarding time and error rates by walking new employees through best practices step by step, encoding experienced workers' knowledge into repeatable processes.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
